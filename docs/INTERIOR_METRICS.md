# Starfall — House Interior Kit: Metrics & Grid Snapping

Generated by `scripts/blender/gen_interiors.py` (see `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` for
the shared style contract). Every model is a flat-shaded, palette-only GLB with
no textures, exported **Y-up** (Blender Z-up → glTF Y-up). In the table below,
dimensions are given as **width (X) × depth (Z) × height (Y)** in glTF space —
equivalently X × Y × Z in Blender. **1 tile = 1.0 unit** and every asset's base
sits exactly at `y = 0` (`z = 0` in Blender). Standalone pieces are centred on
`(0, 0)`; walls and the door are centred on X.

Triangle counts are actual mesh loop-triangles (`tris(col)` in the generator).
The exporter's `EXPORTED … N tris` log line uses `polygons × 2`, which slightly
overcounts n-gon caps on cylinders/cones (e.g. the lantern logs 160 vs 128).
Every standalone asset is well under the 500-triangle budget; the assembled
room is under its 1500-triangle allowance.

## Assembling a room from the pieces

1. **Floor.** Place one `interior_floor` tile at each integer `(x, z)` cell of
   the room footprint. `house_interior` uses a single 4×4 slab (same volume as
   16 tiles) to save triangles. Floor top is at `y = 0.1`; all furniture bases
   are at `y = 0`, so drop furniture by `-0.1` if you want it resting on the
   floor surface rather than the grid plane. The example leaves furniture at
   the grid plane and reads correctly at diorama scale.
2. **Walls.** Each wall is one 1 u grid edge. Put a wall segment at the centre
   of every boundary edge, rotated 90° for edges running along Z. For an
   N×N room you need `4N` segments. The wall is 0.15 u thick, centred on the
   grid line, so half of it sits inside the room and half outside.
3. **Openings.** Swap one wall segment for `interior_door` (0.9 u wide fits the
   1 u gap) and fill the space above it with a 2.5−2.0 = 0.5 u transom; the
   example does exactly this in the south wall. `interior_window` drops into a
   segment the same way — lift it in Y to set the sill height.
4. **Furniture.** Snap pieces to tile centres: `interior_bed` covers 2×1 cells
   with its headboard at −X, `interior_fireplace` takes one tile of flat wall
   frontage, `interior_table` + `interior_chair` form a dining cluster (rotate
   the chair to face the table), and `interior_shelf` sits against a side wall
   (rotate −90° so its front faces into the room). `interior_chest`,
   `interior_rug` and `interior_lantern` are free props.
5. **Verticality.** `interior_stairs` climbs 1.5 u over a 1.5 u run — use two
   flights (mirrored) to reach a 3 u upper storey.

`assets/models/house_interior.glb` is the assembled demo: 4×4 floor, 15 wall
segments plus a door/transom, fireplace, bed, table, chair, shelf. The preview
in `assets/previews/interiors.png` renders its front wall lowered for a
dollhouse cutaway so the furniture is visible — **the exported GLB keeps all
four walls intact**.
